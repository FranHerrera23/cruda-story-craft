The work I was born in Dehradun — a quiet valley town in India, framed by two mountain ranges, with the Himalayas watching from afar. My childhood was simple. No silver spoons or shortcuts. Just stillness, nature, and a quiet dream that maybe, someday, I'd do something meaningful. At nineteen I stepped into a world far removed from mine: Kempinski, Mumbai. My first break in luxury hospitality. Two and a half years that changed everything. Then came Four Seasons. Eight years across the Maldives, Boston, the Bahamas, and later Toronto, the HQ. It wasn't just a job; it was a masterclass in empathy, detail, and connection. India called me home. I joined the leadership program at Taj, and soon after I was entrusted with leading their Maldives resort as General Manager. That resort had survived the tsunami. What it needed wasn't a renovation — it needed a revival of spirit. We built from the inside out. The resort went on to be named No. 1 in the world. True hospitality isn't about luxury. It's about how you make people feel. At Taj Falaknuma we grew the average stay from under one night to over six. At JW Marriott Pune we crossed $24 million in revenue in under a year — a first for a non-metro hotel in South Asia. I've lived in 14 cities across 6 countries. Led turnarounds. Repositioned icons. But what I remember most are the people. And that's why this next chapter feels so right. I've joined Sheikh Shakhbout Medical City in Abu Dhabi as Chief Patient Experience Officer. Here, a warm smile might mean hope. A few extra minutes with a family might bring peace. Every detail matters — not for TripAdvisor reviews, but because someone's loved one is counting on it. Because hospitality is not an industry. It's a mindset. A way of showing up — with empathy, with dignity, with humanity.
